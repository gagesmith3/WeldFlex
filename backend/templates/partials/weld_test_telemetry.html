{# Live feedback for a weld-test run. The controller exposes no way to read a Lua print(), so everything here is what can actually be sampled over RPC while programs/weld.lua executes: program state, the executing line, the phase weld.lua published, contact force, tool Z, and the two weld interlock inputs. The data-* attributes on the root are what weld_test.html's trace log reads on each swap — keep them in sync with the panel above them. Phase is the tile to read first. weld.lua writes a phase code to controller system variable 1 at every transition, and that is the only signal that keeps reporting while force control owns the sensor and the force read is refused. Its sub-line carries the raw return value of the last FT_* instruction and the collision guard the press got (system variable 5). "guard not applied" means neither collision-threshold instruction exists on this firmware, which matters because a 20 lbf press is 89% of the controller's 0-100 N collision scale and reads as a crash to the joint-torque monitor without it. Press travel is the tile to read second, and it is why the press is diagnosable at all. There is no force to show during the press — controller Lua has no force-read instruction and RPC force reads are refused — so travel measured from the contact plane weld.lua published (system variable 3) stands in for it. Settling near 13 mm means the regulator had real force to work with; pinned at its budget means it drove blind and the sensor number is wrong. System variables survive the program, so this stays readable after a fault until the arm moves. Both inputs get their own tile on purpose. weld.lua gates the pulse on both, so collapsing them into one "IO ok" indicator would leave a stall at WELD with no way to tell "stud not seated" from "welder not charged". Poll rate adapts (fast while running, slow when idle) the same way current_job.html does, because each tick costs one batched robot round trip. #}
